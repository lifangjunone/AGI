# AI-SPEC - Phase 4: Local Speech Models

> AI design contract generated by `/gsd-ai-integration-phase`. Consumed by
> `gsd-planner` and `gsd-eval-auditor`.

## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Hybrid (local ASR + TTS inference service)

**Description:**
EasySay sends locally recorded English speech through a loopback-only inference
service for transcription and synthesizes lesson and coach text as natural
English audio. Good behavior means accurate accented-English transcription,
clear low-latency speech, private local processing, observable model state, and
automatic browser fallback.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. Audio or model configuration leaves the local machine unexpectedly.
2. A failed model request blocks recording, transcript editing, or playback.
3. ASR silently replaces a useful transcript with empty or malformed output.
4. TTS returns corrupt, wrong-language, or misleading audio.
5. Model reloads exhaust memory or allow concurrent unsafe inference.

## 1b. Domain Context

**Industry Vertical:** Adult second-language learning and computer-assisted
English speaking practice

**User Population:** Adult Chinese learners of English across proficiency
levels, including speakers whose intelligible English retains Chinese-accented
phonetic, rhythm, or prosodic features

**Stakes Level:** High

**Output Consequence:** ASR transcripts become evidence for speaking feedback
and retry decisions, while TTS provides the pronunciation model learners hear
and imitate. Errors can teach the wrong form, erase what a learner actually
said, penalize an intelligible accent, or interrupt daily practice. This is not
a certified language test or clinical assessment, and users can edit
transcripts and use browser fallback, which limits but does not remove harm.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

**Dimension: Learner meaning and error preservation**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** The transcript preserves the words the
learner actually produced, including genuine grammatical or lexical errors,
and correctly captures meaning-bearing content words. Reviewers inspect
substitutions, deletions, and insertions separately; deletion of a key word or
substitution that changes meaning is treated more seriously than punctuation,
capitalization, or a harmless function-word difference.  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** The ASR silently rewrites hesitant or
non-native speech into a more fluent sentence, drops negation, tense or key
content, invents words during pauses, or returns blank text for audible speech.
Such output cannot support valid learner feedback even when aggregate word
error rate looks acceptable.  
**Stakes:** High  
**Source:** Second-language ASR research and pronunciation-assessment practice;
ASR transcription accuracy must be interpreted against human intelligibility,
task type, language background, and error consequences rather than WER alone.

**Dimension: Fair recognition of Chinese-accented English**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** Chinese-accented speech that adult
listeners understand receives a usable transcript without requiring a
native-like accent. Acceptance is checked separately for read and spontaneous
speech, multiple proficiency bands, speakers and genders, short hesitations,
self-corrections, varied speaking rates, and ordinary laptop-room noise.
Reviewers compare subgroup substitution/deletion rates and meaning-changing
errors, not only one pooled average.  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Stronger accent, lower proficiency,
spontaneous speech, or a speaker subgroup causes a systematic rise in omitted
or meaning-changing words while native or near-native samples pass. Accent
features are treated as mistakes despite the utterance remaining intelligible.
  
**Stakes:** High  
**Source:** Fair-ASR studies report large WER variation across second-language
accent groups; pronunciation-assessment research distinguishes intelligibility
and comprehensibility from native-likeness or accentedness.

**Dimension: TTS intelligibility for adult Chinese learners**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** Learners can transcribe and act on the
intended words without seeing the text, including lesson-critical minimal
contrasts, names, numbers, contractions, and sentence boundaries. Pace,
chunking, prominence, and pauses support comprehension at the target level
without uniformly slowing or distorting the utterance. Both American and
British voices are tested by Chinese L1 listeners across proficiency bands.
  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Learners mishear lesson-critical words,
word boundaries disappear, stress changes meaning, homographs or numbers are
mispronounced, or fast delivery makes otherwise correct audio unusable.
Passing an ASR-back-transcription check alone is insufficient because machine
recognition can use different cues from L2 listeners.  
**Stakes:** High  
**Source:** L2-tailored clear-speech research finds that measured listener
transcription can diverge from perceived clarity and that ASR is not a
sufficient proxy for L2-listener intelligibility.

**Dimension: Natural, trustworthy pronunciation model**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** A trained listener judges segmental
pronunciation, lexical and sentence stress, rhythm, intonation, and phrasing as
coherent natural English for the selected voice. Audio is free of clicks,
truncation, repeated fragments, unstable volume, wrong-language output, and
abrupt joins; clarity does not become patronizing over-enunciation.  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** The words are recoverable but the voice is
robotic, has misleading stress or intonation, changes speaker characteristics
within an utterance, repeats or skips content, or models a pronunciation a
teacher would not ask learners to imitate.  
**Stakes:** High  
**Source:** Speech-synthesis practice separates intelligibility from
naturalness and prosody; human listening judgments remain necessary because
objective speech metrics correlate imperfectly with perception.

**Dimension: Private and recoverable speaking practice**  
**Good (domain expert would accept):** With networking observed during a test,
audio, transcripts, lesson text, and model configuration stay on the device;
the service binds only to loopback and logs metadata rather than content.
Stopping, misconfiguring, timing out, or exhausting the local service produces
a visible diagnostic and promptly returns control to transcript editing,
recording, or browser speech fallback without losing the learner's take.
  
**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Speech or text reaches a remote endpoint,
raw content appears in logs or caches without a retention purpose, diagnostics
expose recordings, or a model failure leaves the learner waiting, overwrites
their recording/transcript, or disables the practice loop.  
**Stakes:** Critical  
**Source:** Privacy-by-design and data-minimization principles for voice data,
plus EasySay requirements SPCH-03 through SPCH-05 and the phase's explicit
local-processing promise.

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **Fluency laundering:** ASR replaces false starts, learner grammar, or an
   intended but imperfect phrase with a plausible fluent sentence, so later
   feedback evaluates the recognizer's correction rather than the learner.
2. **Accent-skewed substitution and deletion:** Chinese-accented segments,
   reduced final consonants, stress patterns, connected speech, or slower
   delivery trigger disproportionate deletion of content words or substitution
   by a more probable phrase, especially in spontaneous speech.
3. **Misleading pronunciation model:** TTS says the right text with wrong
   lexical stress, unnatural rhythm, ambiguous boundaries, excessive speed, or
   an artifact that learners may copy; average naturalness can conceal a
   lesson-critical word failure.
4. **Silent local-service failure:** Empty ASR, corrupt or wrong-language TTS,
   model reload, memory pressure, or a stalled request is accepted as success
   and blocks the edit-retry loop instead of preserving the take and invoking
   browser fallback.
5. **Privacy-promise breach:** A loopback service, diagnostic script, telemetry,
   cache, or log unexpectedly exports or retains identifiable voice or lesson
   content, contradicting the reason for choosing local inference.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

No education-sector certification or regulated language-score use is identified
for this adult, local-first deployment. Voice recordings and linked transcripts
are personal data. If EasySay is distributed beyond the current single-device
context, the applicable privacy regime depends on user location: China PIPL and
EU/UK GDPR can require a disclosed purpose, data minimization, retention and
deletion controls, security, and a lawful basis. Under GDPR, voice becomes
special-category biometric data when technically processed to uniquely
identify a person; EasySay must not add voice identification without a separate
legal and product review. Local-only processing, no raw-content logging, and
user-controlled deletion are acceptance criteria even where no specific
statute has yet been selected.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** mlx-audio 0.3.1 + FastAPI

**Version:** `mlx-audio==0.3.1`; FastAPI `>=0.116,<1`

**Rationale:**
`mlx-audio` is native to Apple Silicon and directly supports the selected Qwen3
ASR/TTS MLX models. FastAPI provides a small typed loopback service that the
existing Express API can proxy without introducing an agent framework.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| Hugging Face Transformers + PyTorch MPS + FastAPI | Larger unquantized weights, less predictable Apple Silicon support, and model cards focus on CUDA. |
| vLLM | Current production path assumes CUDA and is not a macOS runtime. |
| LangChain / LangGraph | Deterministic speech inference does not need agent orchestration. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Partial. Model adapters are Qwen/MLX specific, but
the Express-facing ASR/TTS contract and browser fallbacks remain replaceable.

### Core Abstractions

| Abstraction | Phase 4 use |
|---|---|
| `mlx_audio.stt.utils.load_model()` | Lazily or eagerly load the 1.7B ASR model once per service process. |
| `Qwen3ASRModel.generate()` | Deterministic English transcription; returns `STTOutput` with text, segments, token counts, timing, and throughput. |
| `mlx_audio.tts.utils.load_model()` | Lazily or eagerly load the CustomVoice TTS conversion once. |
| `model.generate()` | Yield `GenerationResult` chunks containing audio, sample rate, processing time, and peak MLX memory. |
| PyAV + SoundFile | Decode browser WebM/Opus to mono 16 kHz and encode PCM WAV responses. |

### Required Pitfalls

1. **Do not block the event loop.** Both model loaders and generation methods
   are synchronous. Run all MLX work on one dedicated executor thread.
2. **Do not infer concurrently on shared models.** v0.3.1 has no model-level
   inference lock and calls `mx.clear_cache()` during generation. Serialize ASR
   and TTS behind one `asyncio.Lock`; run Uvicorn with one worker.
3. **Do not load per request or per process.** Multiple Uvicorn workers duplicate
   both weight sets in unified memory and cold downloads can stall requests.
4. **Do not confuse a Python generator with async streaming.** TTS and streaming
   ASR return synchronous generators. Iterating them in an `async def` route
   still blocks unless iteration happens on the dedicated worker.
5. **Do not promise upstream benchmark latency.** Qwen reports TTS first-packet
   latency as low as 97 ms for its reference stack, not this 8-bit MLX service.
   Measure cold load, queue wait, first audio, total time, and peak memory on the
   target M5 Pro.
6. **Do not silently accept empty output.** Reject blank ASR text for non-silent
   input and zero-length/non-finite TTS audio, then preserve browser fallback.
7. **WAV is the service response format.** PyAV decodes browser WebM/Opus and
   SoundFile writes PCM WAV, avoiding a system `ffmpeg` dependency.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

### Runtime Ownership and Entry Point

Use one long-lived service process bound only to loopback. A dedicated
single-thread executor gives model load and inference stable ownership while
keeping FastAPI responsive; the async lock makes queueing explicit:

```python
# speech/runtime.py
import asyncio
import io
from concurrent.futures import ThreadPoolExecutor
from functools import partial

import numpy as np
from mlx_audio.audio_io import read as audio_read
from mlx_audio.audio_io import write as audio_write
from mlx_audio.stt import load as load_stt
from mlx_audio.stt.utils import resample_audio
from mlx_audio.tts.utils import load_model as load_tts

ASR_MODEL = "mlx-community/Qwen3-ASR-1.7B-8bit"
TTS_MODEL = "mlx-community/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-CustomVoice-8bit"


class SpeechRuntime:
    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self._worker = ThreadPoolExecutor(max_workers=1, thread_name_prefix="mlx")
        self._gate = asyncio.Lock()
        self.asr = None
        self.tts = None

    async def _run(self, func, /, *args, **kwargs):
        loop = asyncio.get_running_loop()
        call = partial(func, *args, **kwargs)
        return await loop.run_in_executor(self._worker, call)

    async def start(self) -> None:
        async with self._gate:
            self.asr, self.tts = await self._run(
                lambda: (load_stt(ASR_MODEL), load_tts(TTS_MODEL))
            )
            supported = {name.lower() for name in self.tts.get_supported_speakers()}
            if not {"aiden", "ryan"} <= supported:
                raise RuntimeError(f"Required voices unavailable: {sorted(supported)}")

    def _transcribe(self, payload: bytes) -> dict:
        samples, sample_rate = audio_read(io.BytesIO(payload), always_2d=True)
        mono = samples.astype(np.float32).mean(axis=1)
        if sample_rate != self.asr.sample_rate:
            mono = resample_audio(mono, sample_rate, self.asr.sample_rate)
        result = self.asr.generate(
            mono,
            language="English",
            temperature=0.0,
            max_tokens=512,
            chunk_duration=30.0,
        )
        return {
            "text": result.text.strip(),
            "segments": result.segments or [],
            "total_time_ms": round(result.total_time * 1000),
        }

    async def transcribe(self, payload: bytes) -> dict:
        async with self._gate:
            return await self._run(self._transcribe, payload)

    def _synthesize(self, text: str, voice: str, instruct: str | None) -> bytes:
        results = list(
            self.tts.generate_custom_voice(
                text=text,
                speaker=voice,
                language="English",
                instruct=instruct,
                temperature=0.8,
                top_k=50,
                top_p=1.0,
                repetition_penalty=1.05,
                max_tokens=2048,
            )
        )
        if not results:
            raise RuntimeError("TTS returned no audio")
        chunks = [np.asarray(item.audio.tolist(), dtype=np.float32) for item in results]
        audio = np.concatenate(chunks)
        if audio.size == 0 or not np.isfinite(audio).all():
            raise RuntimeError("TTS returned invalid audio")
        output = io.BytesIO()
        audio_write(output, audio, results[0].sample_rate, format="wav")
        return output.getvalue()

    async def synthesize(
        self, text: str, voice: str, instruct: str | None
    ) -> bytes:
        async with self._gate:
            return await self._run(self._synthesize, text, voice, instruct)

    def close(self) -> None:
        self._worker.shutdown(wait=True, cancel_futures=True)
```

```python
# speech/app.py
from contextlib import asynccontextmanager

from fastapi import FastAPI, File, HTTPException, Response, UploadFile

from speech.runtime import SpeechRuntime
from speech.schemas import ASRResponse, TTSRequest

runtime = SpeechRuntime()


@asynccontextmanager
async def lifespan(_: FastAPI):
    await runtime.start()
    yield
    runtime.close()


app = FastAPI(lifespan=lifespan)


@app.get("/health")
async def health() -> dict[str, object]:
    return {"ready": runtime.asr is not None and runtime.tts is not None}


@app.post("/v1/asr", response_model=ASRResponse)
async def transcribe(file: UploadFile = File(...)) -> ASRResponse:
    try:
        return ASRResponse.model_validate(await runtime.transcribe(await file.read()))
    except (RuntimeError, ValueError) as exc:
        raise HTTPException(status_code=422, detail=str(exc)) from exc


@app.post("/v1/tts")
async def synthesize(request: TTSRequest) -> Response:
    try:
        wav = await runtime.synthesize(
            request.text, request.voice, request.instruct
        )
        return Response(wav, media_type="audio/wav")
    except (RuntimeError, ValueError) as exc:
        raise HTTPException(status_code=422, detail=str(exc)) from exc
```

Run exactly one process:

```bash
uv run uvicorn speech.app:app \
  --host 127.0.0.1 --port 8765 --workers 1
```

Node/Express remains the browser-facing boundary. It should use a short
connect timeout, a bounded end-to-end timeout, and browser ASR/TTS fallback.
Timing out an `asyncio` waiter does not stop an already-running executor call;
do not release the gate or start replacement inference until that call exits.

### Model Parameters and State

- ASR: `language="English"`, `temperature=0.0`, `max_tokens=512` for short
  recordings, and `chunk_duration=30.0`. Reject oversized uploads before decode.
- TTS: `language="English"`, `temperature=0.8`, `top_k=50`, `top_p=1.0`,
  `repetition_penalty=1.05`, and `max_tokens=2048`. Cap input text and split
  long lessons at sentence boundaries in the application layer.
- State: models are immutable process-owned objects. Configuration changes are
  validated and persisted first, then applied by a controlled service restart;
  never hot-swap a model while inference is running.
- Prompt/tool configuration: there are no tools or conversational prompts.
  The only instruction-like input is the bounded TTS style `instruct`; keep it
  server-generated or allowlisted rather than accepting arbitrary browser text.

### Latency and Memory Budget

The conversion cards are approximately 2.47 GB (ASR) and 3.08 GB (TTS) on
disk. Unified-memory residency is higher because weights, tokenizer state,
audio features, KV caches, and generated buffers coexist. Do not use the
5.55 GB file total as a RAM estimate.

On the 48 GB M5 Pro, preload both models and retain at least 12 GB of system
headroom. Establish measured gates after a warm-up request: ASR real-time
factor for 5/15/30-second clips, TTS time-to-first/complete WAV for
sentence/paragraph inputs, p95 queue wait, process RSS, and MLX-reported peak
memory. Initial product budgets, to be validated rather than treated as vendor
claims, are p95 ASR completion within audio duration for clips up to 30 seconds,
p95 short-sentence TTS completion within 2 seconds, and no monotonic memory
growth across 50 serialized mixed requests. If these fail, first reduce text or
audio limits; evaluate 0.6B models only as an explicit quality/latency tradeoff.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

### 4b.1 Structured Outputs with Pydantic

This is deterministic speech inference, not an LLM structured-output API.
Pydantic validates the HTTP boundary; adapter code must convert and validate
mlx-audio dataclasses before returning them:

```python
# speech/schemas.py
from typing import Annotated, Literal

from pydantic import BaseModel, ConfigDict, Field


class Segment(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid")
    text: str
    start: Annotated[float, Field(ge=0)]
    end: Annotated[float, Field(ge=0)]


class ASRResponse(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid")
    text: Annotated[str, Field(min_length=1)]
    segments: list[Segment] = Field(default_factory=list)
    total_time_ms: Annotated[int, Field(ge=0)]


class TTSRequest(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid", str_strip_whitespace=True)
    text: Annotated[str, Field(min_length=1, max_length=2000)]
    voice: Literal["Aiden", "Ryan"] = "Aiden"
    instruct: Annotated[str | None, Field(max_length=240)] = None
```

Validate once after inference. Retry at most once only for a classified
transient failure such as an empty/non-finite TTS result or a one-time model
load failure; do not retry invalid input, unsupported voices, decode errors, or
timeouts. Log request ID, model ID, attempt, input duration/text length, queue
wait, inference time, output size, and exception class, never raw audio or
lesson text. After the retry, surface a typed 422/503 so Express can fall back.

### 4b.2 Async-First Design

FastAPI's event loop handles uploads, health checks, and responses; all
synchronous MLX load/generation and generator iteration runs on the one
dedicated worker. The common failure is calling `model.generate()` directly
inside `async def`, which freezes every route. Also never call `asyncio.run()`
from a route because Uvicorn already owns the event loop.

Use complete `await` results for ASR replacement and validated WAV responses.
Add streaming only if UX measurements require it: bridge the synchronous
generator to an async bounded queue from the dedicated worker, propagate
disconnects, and keep the inference gate held until generator cleanup
completes. Streaming improves perceived latency but does not permit a second
MLX inference.

### 4b.3 Input and Instruction Discipline

ASR receives audio plus fixed server-side `language="English"`; TTS separates
lesson text from the style instruction. Do not concatenate browser-provided
metadata into `instruct`. Keep a small allowlist of reviewed style strings for
lesson, coach, American, and British modes. No few-shot examples or dynamic
prompt retrieval are needed.

Set generation bounds explicitly: ASR `max_tokens=512`, TTS
`max_tokens=2048`, upload duration/byte limits, TTS text length, and queue
depth. Never expose unbounded mlx-audio defaults (`8192` ASR and `4096` TTS) to
browser-controlled requests.

### 4b.4 Audio and Generation Context Management

There is no chat context window. The equivalent pressure points are audio
duration and generated codec tokens. Normalize uploads to mono 16 kHz for ASR,
reject corrupt or over-limit media, and chunk only after enforcing a total
duration cap. For TTS, split long text at sentence boundaries, synthesize
sequentially, and concatenate only chunks with the same sample rate. Do not
carry ASR tokens, TTS caches, or prior style instructions across requests.

### 4b.5 Cost and Latency Budget

Inference has zero provider API cost and keeps audio local; machine time,
memory, disk, and user-visible delay are the budget. Cache TTS by a versioned
exact key over model revision, text, canonical voice, style, and generation
parameters. Do not use semantic caching for speech because near-matching text
can change pronunciation or meaning. Avoid persistent ASR caching by default;
if needed, use a local content hash with the same retention policy as the
recording.

Track cold-load time separately from warm inference and expose queue depth so
the Node proxy can fail fast to browser speech rather than accumulate work.
The selected 1.7B models remain the quality path; a 0.6B route is acceptable
only after accent WER and voice-quality evaluation demonstrates that its
latency gain is worth the regression.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

Evaluation is product-level and runs against the pinned
`mlx-community/Qwen3-ASR-1.7B-8bit` and
`mlx-community/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-CustomVoice-8bit` snapshots through the
FastAPI/Express path. Generic model-card scores are evidence for selection, not
release evidence for EasySay.

### Release Rubrics

| Priority | Dimension and gate | Concrete rubric | Measurement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | Private, recoverable practice | **PASS:** packet capture shows only loopback traffic; logs contain IDs, sizes, timings, model revision, and error classes but no audio, transcript, or lesson text; every stopped, timed-out, invalid, or empty-output path preserves the recording/editable transcript and activates browser fallback. **FAIL:** content leaves the device, appears in logs, is overwritten, or a model failure blocks recording/editing/playback. | Code + privacy reviewer |
| Critical | Learner meaning and error preservation | **PASS:** normalized aggregate WER is `<=20%`, spontaneous-speech WER is `<=28%`, meaning-bearing keyword recall is `>=95%`, and all labeled negations, numbers, names, self-corrections, and genuine learner errors are preserved verbatim. No audible sample returns blank text. **FAIL:** ASR fluency-launders an error, drops or changes any critical meaning token, invents speech in a labeled pause, or replaces useful text with empty/malformed output. | Code (`jiwer` plus labeled token/error checks) + annotator adjudication |
| High | Fair Chinese-accented English recognition | **PASS:** intelligible Chinese-accented read and spontaneous samples pass the same meaning gates; no proficiency, gender, task, or speaker subgroup has deletion WER more than `10` percentage points above the paired reference subgroup. **FAIL:** an intelligible accent is penalized as an error or one subgroup shows systematic content-word deletion/substitution hidden by pooled WER. | Code by fixture metadata + applied linguist review |
| Critical | TTS intelligibility and text fidelity | **PASS:** ASR loopback normalized WER is `<=10%` as a smoke check, while blind Chinese-L1 listener transcription is `>=95%` by word and `100%` for labeled lesson-critical words, names, numbers, contractions, and minimal contrasts; intended sentence boundaries are recoverable. **FAIL:** any critical item is misheard, omitted, repeated, wrong-language, or ambiguous even if loopback ASR passes. | Code loopback + human listening |
| High | Natural, trustworthy pronunciation model | **PASS:** for both Aiden and Ryan, median P.800-style MOS is `>=4.0/5`, every sample is `>=3/5`, and trained review finds no misleading lexical stress, rhythm, intonation, clipping, clicks, speaker drift, repeated fragments, or patronizing over-enunciation. **FAIL:** recoverable words are still an unsuitable pronunciation model or any critical artifact is present. | Human: EFL teacher, phonetician, and Chinese-L1 learners |
| High | Task completion and API validity | **PASS:** ASR returns schema-valid non-empty English text; TTS returns finite, non-empty, decodable PCM WAV; Aiden/Ryan selection, configuration apply/test, single-request serialization, and Express fallback satisfy SPCH-01..05. **FAIL:** a malformed response is accepted, requests overlap shared MLX state, or any required workflow cannot complete. | Code integration tests |
| High | M5 Pro latency and memory | **PASS:** after one warm-up on the 48 GB M5 Pro, p95 ASR real-time factor is `<=1.0` for 5/15/30-second clips, p95 TTS completion is `<=2.0 s` for sentences up to 140 characters and `<=8.0 s` for 500-character paragraphs, p95 queue wait is `<=250 ms` with one active user, peak process RSS plus MLX allocations is `<=36 GB`, and 50 serialized mixed requests grow steady-state memory by `<=1 GB`. **FAIL:** any gate is exceeded, inference overlaps, or memory grows monotonically. | Code benchmark (`time`, `psutil`, MLX peak memory) |

Human scores use a blinded 1–5 rubric: `5` fully intelligible/natural with no
effort or defect; `4` clear and suitable with only a minor non-misleading
defect; `3` understandable but noticeably effortful or pedagogically
questionable; `2` partially intelligible or misleading; `1` unusable. Each TTS
fixture receives at least three ratings, including one adult Chinese learner
and one EFL teacher/phonetician. Any lesson-critical disagreement is
adjudicated rather than averaged away. An LLM judge is intentionally excluded:
it cannot hear the waveform reliably or substitute for L2 listener judgments.

### Reference Dataset

Create `tests/speech/fixtures/manifest.jsonl` during implementation and keep
small consented/CC-licensed WAV fixtures in Git LFS or an ignored,
checksum-verified local bundle. Start with **16 samples**:

- 8 ASR recordings: two read and two spontaneous Chinese-accented samples
  spanning lower/higher proficiency and different speakers/genders; one
  near-native control; one ordinary-room-noise sample; one
  hesitation/self-correction/learner-error sample; and one critical
  negation/names/numbers sample.
- 6 TTS texts: short lesson sentence, coach reply, minimal contrasts, names and
  numbers, contractions/homograph/stress case, and a 500-character paragraph.
  Render each relevant item with Aiden and Ryan; human labels record exact
  transcription, critical-token accuracy, MOS, listening effort, and defects.
- 2 failure fixtures: silence/corrupt or oversized audio and a forced
  unavailable/timeout model path, used to prove typed failure and browser
  fallback without data loss.

Every row records fixture ID, checksum, consent/license, task, speaker
pseudonym, proficiency band, accent, gender bucket where consented, acoustic
condition, verbatim transcript with disfluency conventions, critical tokens,
expected status/fallback, and model/config revision. A speech annotator labels
references; the applied linguist/EFL teacher adjudicates meaning-changing
errors. Expand toward 20 samples only from observed failures, without silently
changing the frozen release set.

### Tooling and CI

Use `pytest`, `jiwer`, `soundfile`, and `psutil` with versioned JSON/JSONL
results; install eval-only dependencies with:

```bash
.venv-speech/bin/python -m pip install pytest jiwer psutil
```

Arize Phoenix is explicitly **not** adopted despite the generic tracing
default. This service has no prompt, retrieval, agent graph, remote provider,
or semantic trace to inspect. Phoenix would add a daemon, dependencies, storage
and a new privacy surface around voice metadata without improving deterministic
WER, WAV validity, latency, memory, or fallback checks. Lightweight structured
metrics and local eval artifacts are the proportionate observability override.

Run model-free contract/failure tests on every CI job:

```bash
npm run typecheck && npm test
.venv-speech/bin/python -m pytest tests/speech -m "not model" -q
```

Run the pinned-model release gate on a self-hosted M5 Pro 48 GB runner after
warm-up; it emits `artifacts/speech-eval.json` and fails on any code threshold:

```bash
.venv-speech/bin/python -m pytest tests/speech -m model -q \
  --speech-manifest tests/speech/fixtures/manifest.jsonl \
  --speech-eval-output artifacts/speech-eval.json
```

Human MOS/intelligibility labels are a required signed release artifact when a
model snapshot, voice, generation parameter, decoder, or audio pipeline
changes. CI validates that the labels match the current fixture and model
checksums; it does not manufacture subjective scores.

## 6. Guardrails

### Online Guardrails

| Failure mode | Every-request guardrail | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Privacy-promise breach | Validate configured URL/host as `127.0.0.1`, `localhost`, or `::1`; bind Uvicorn to loopback; disable content-bearing tracing; structured logger rejects raw body, transcript, and text fields. | Refuse startup/config save on non-loopback values; emit a content-free error counter. |
| Invalid or hostile input | Enforce content type, `25 MB` and duration caps, decodability, mono normalization, non-silence for ASR, `1..2000` TTS characters, English mode, canonical Aiden/Ryan voices, and allowlisted server-side style instructions. | Return typed `400/413/422`; do not invoke MLX; preserve the learner take and use manual/browser path. |
| Silent bad ASR/TTS output | Reject blank ASR for non-silent input; schema-validate text; require finite non-empty audio, one supported sample rate, decodable PCM WAV, plausible duration, and no clipped/truncated response. | Count validation failure, discard only the generated output, retain source recording/transcript, and trigger browser fallback. |
| Stalled or unsafe inference | One process, one shared async lock, bounded queue depth of one waiting request, explicit connect/end-to-end timeout, and no reload while inference owns the model. | Fail fast with `503`, keep the lock until the worker exits, then fall back; never launch replacement inference concurrently. |
| Broken practice loop | Express and UI treat local `4xx/5xx`, timeout, disconnect, and service-offline states as recoverable capability failures. | ASR leaves existing/manual transcript editable; TTS calls `speechSynthesis`; recording and retry remain enabled with a visible diagnostic. |

These checks are intentionally local and constant-time except audio decoding and
WAV validation, which are already required to process a request.

### Offline Flywheel

| Signal | Review and response |
|---|---|
| WER, critical-token error, blank/repetition, and subgroup deletion rates | Run the frozen set on every release; add a redacted, consented reproduction when a production failure reveals a missing category. Block release on rubric failure. |
| Human TTS intelligibility, MOS, listening effort, and defect tags | Re-score all affected TTS fixtures on model/voice/parameter changes and quarterly while actively shipping; investigate any score below the gate. |
| Fallback, retry, timeout, queue rejection, and user transcript-edit-after-ASR rates | Review weekly by model/config revision. Rising fallback or heavy post-ASR editing triggers fixture capture and root-cause analysis. |
| Latency and memory drift | Run the 50-request M5 Pro soak at release; compare p50/p95 and peak/growth against the last accepted artifact. |
| Privacy controls | Before release, inspect listeners with `lsof`, observe network traffic during ASR/TTS, scan structured logs for fixture canaries, and verify local recording/cache deletion behavior. |

## 7. Production Monitoring

Production observability is a local, content-free metrics snapshot exposed
through the loopback health endpoint and optionally persisted as rotating
JSONL. Labels are bounded to operation, outcome/error class, model revision,
voice, and config revision; never use transcript/text, file path, speaker,
audio hash, or user identity as a label.

### Counters, Histograms, and Gauges

| Metric | Threshold / action |
|---|---|
| `speech_requests_total{operation,outcome}` and `speech_errors_total{operation,class}` | Alert in the settings UI if errors exceed `5%` over 20 requests or any rolling 15-minute window; split decode, validation, timeout, load, and inference failures. |
| `speech_fallback_total{operation,reason}` and `speech_fallback_success_total` | Every failed local request must have a corresponding successful fallback or explicit user-visible manual path; alert on any unmatched event. |
| `speech_empty_asr_total`, `speech_invalid_wav_total`, `speech_wrong_language_total`, `speech_repeated_audio_total` | Any occurrence is release-review evidence; two occurrences for one model revision disable that local operation until reviewed. |
| `speech_latency_ms{operation}` and `speech_queue_wait_ms` | Keep p50/p95 plus bounded buckets. Warn when p95 ASR RTF exceeds `1.0`, sentence TTS exceeds `2 s`, paragraph TTS exceeds `8 s`, or queue wait exceeds `250 ms` over 20 requests. |
| `speech_input_duration_seconds`, `speech_tts_input_chars`, and `speech_output_duration_seconds` | Histograms only, for normalization and implausible-duration detection; no content values. |
| `speech_fallback_latency_ms` and `speech_transcript_edited_total{magnitude_bucket}` | Rising fallback delay or edits greater than 20% of ASR words are quality signals for offline review, not proof of a bad transcript. |
| `speech_model_load_total{operation,outcome}`, `speech_model_load_ms`, `speech_model_loaded`, and `speech_reload_total{outcome}` | Show readiness immediately; alert after one failed load/reload and keep browser fallback active. |
| `speech_process_rss_bytes`, `speech_mlx_peak_bytes`, `speech_queue_depth`, and `speech_inflight` | Warn above `34 GB`, reject/disable local inference at `36 GB`, and alert if `inflight > 1`, queue depth exceeds one, or steady-state growth exceeds `1 GB/50` requests. |
| `speech_non_loopback_attempt_total` and `speech_content_log_rejection_total` | Security counters: any non-zero value disables the affected configuration and requires privacy review. |

All requests contribute code metrics because the counters are cheap and contain
no speech content. The health UI shows current readiness, request/error/fallback
counts, last and rolling p95 latency, queue depth, and memory headroom. Keep
rotating metrics for 30 days locally, resettable by the user.

Quality sampling is signal-weighted: review all invalid-output, timeout,
fallback, large transcript-edit, and memory-pressure events using only their
metadata; request explicit consent before retaining the associated audio/text
as a new fixture. During active releases, randomly solicit a local
intelligibility/MOS rating for at most 1% of successful TTS plays, with no
automatic content upload. Weekly review compares model/config revisions;
release review compares the frozen dataset and M5 Pro benchmark to the last
accepted baseline.
